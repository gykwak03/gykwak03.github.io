**SS-TI (Sweet & Salty Taste Identifier)** is a browser-based assessment that
classifies combined sweet and salty taste-preference patterns and returns
profile-specific healthy-eating guidance.
The project was developed with Food & Nutrition students to connect questionnaire
design, statistical evaluation, and a complete user-facing web flow rather than
using an informal preference quiz.

The questionnaire contains 40 literature-derived items divided equally between
sugar and sodium. Each domain includes ten food-frequency questions and ten
eating-behavior questions, all measured on a five-point scale. Items were selected
from theses and peer-reviewed studies that reported statistically significant
measures related to intake or dietary behavior. The KMO test was used to assess
whether the item structure was suitable for factor analysis, and Cronbach’s α
was used to examine internal consistency.

The full application was implemented with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Client-side
logic validates responses, calculates separate sweet and salty scores, combines
them into a preference profile, and routes the user to the corresponding result
page. Each result explains the classified pattern and presents practical dietary
recommendations prepared from the nutrition-domain review.

The final system demonstrates a complete path from evidence-based item selection
to statistical questionnaire checks and an interpretable browser result. It is
intended as an educational self-assessment, not as a clinical or diagnostic
instrument.
