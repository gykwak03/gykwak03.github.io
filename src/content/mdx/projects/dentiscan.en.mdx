**DentiScan** is a two-stage screening prototype for suspected caries and tooth
wear in a single oral photograph. A detector first localizes small candidate
regions, and a second classifier reviews each crop to remove regions predicted
as normal. This detect-then-verify design was selected to address uneven lighting,
small lesion boundaries, and normal tooth textures that can resemble disease in
consumer-quality images.

The project used AlphaDent, approximately 1,200 high-resolution photographs from
295 patients. Pixel-level masks were converted into detection labels, and 14,497
cropped images were prepared for four-way classification. Model development
included ablation studies and comparisons between RF-DETR and D-FINE for
detection and between Swin Transformer and RegNet for crop classification.
Because abrasion objects outnumbered caries by 1.76 to 1, Copy-Paste augmentation
increased the number of caries objects by 25% while preserving the surrounding
oral-image context.

The final architecture paired RF-DETR with a Swin Transformer. Copy-Paste
augmentation raised caries recall from **0.3206 to 0.5370 (+67%)**, caries
mAP@0.5:0.95 from 0.1706 to 0.3219, and overall detector recall from 0.5736 to
0.6216. Evaluation on a second dataset produced a marked decline in performance,
indicating sensitivity to image source and acquisition conditions rather than
consistent cross-domain generalization.

The inference pipeline was integrated into a Streamlit application and deployed
on AWS EC2. The result screen reports candidate boxes, confidence scores, lesion
counts, and cropped regions, while explicitly presenting the output as screening
support that requires professional dental assessment rather than as a diagnosis.
