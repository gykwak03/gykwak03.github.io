**DentiScan** is a two-stage screening prototype for suspected caries and tooth
wear in oral photographs. Initial RF-DETR experiments showed weaker detection
of caries than abrasion, motivating augmentation and threshold experiments.

Copy-Paste augmentation increased **caries precision from 56.56% to 60.44%** and
**caries recall from 39.56% to 43.17%**. Combining Copy-Paste with Gamma Correction
and setting the detection confidence threshold to **0.35** raised caries recall
to **48.89%**, a gain of **9.33 percentage points** from baseline. Caries precision
decreased to **50.16%**. The combined configuration was selected to detect more
caries candidates, with subsequent classification intended to filter the
additional false positives.

The pipeline first detects lesion candidates broadly, then removes crops
classified as normal. Detection bounding boxes were compared with ground truth
using IoU to construct the classification dataset. RegNet and Swin Transformer
were compared, and **Swin Transformer** was selected for the second stage.

In the final pipeline evaluation, adding Swin Transformer to RF-DETR increased
**overall precision from 67.41% to 80.63%**, while **overall recall decreased from
73.25% to 62.74%**. This comparison showed that the second stage filtered false
positives while also missing some additional true lesions.

The Streamlit application was deployed on AWS EC2. It displays **confidence
scores for each detected region and their mean**, alongside lesion locations
and counts, so users can inspect the model's confidence in its screening output.
