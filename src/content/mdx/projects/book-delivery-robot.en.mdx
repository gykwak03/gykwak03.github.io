This public-data project proposed priority hubs and routes for a **book-delivery
robot service** in Gwanak District, Seoul, where access to library services
varies across neighborhoods. The analysis treated schools, traditional markets, apartment
complexes, senior centers, and disability facilities as candidate stops so that
the proposed network could serve locations with both public demand and practical
delivery value.

Nine public datasets were cleaned and standardized into comparable geographic
units. Candidate locations were evaluated using four indicators: the number of
libraries within one kilometer, local foot traffic, library-use activity, and
distance to the nearest library. OLS regression was used to estimate the relative
weights of these indicators, converting heterogeneous source data into a single
hub-priority score. Locations with a total score of 45 or higher were retained as
priority candidates.

The selected candidates were grouped geographically, and Folium was used to map
time-dependent stops and proposed delivery paths. Comparing the candidate
clusters identified the **Bongcheon–Seoul National University** area as the most
practical initial service zone based on accessibility and the distribution of
high-priority stops.

The final output combined data preprocessing, statistical weighting, candidate
selection, and geospatial visualization in one reproducible workflow. The result
was framed as a planning study for hub prioritization rather than as an
autonomous-routing system validated in the field.
