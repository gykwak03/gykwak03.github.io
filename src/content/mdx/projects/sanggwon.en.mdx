**Seoul Foodservice Site Intelligence** is a decision-support service for
prospective food-service entrepreneurs comparing vacant sites in Seoul. Originally
developed under the Korean product name *상권을 부탁해*, it connects each listing
to an estimated probability of remaining open for three years, structured search
filters, and an explainable location report. The completed service won the Grand
Prize in the Product & Service Development category within the Startup Division
of the 2026 Seoul Metropolitan Government Big Data Competition and received the
Mayor of Seoul Award.

The prediction target was defined at the cohort level: a food-service business opening in
year Y was labeled according to whether it remained in operation through the end
of Y+3. Four opening cohorts from 2019 to 2022 were constructed so that every
feature reflected information available at the relevant opening-year cutoff.
The data pipeline integrates **16 public datasets** covering licenses,
population, spending, sales, store density, transport, buildings, land prices,
and prior business history. Eleven processing stages use spatial joins and
BallTree neighborhoods at 250, 500, and 1,000 meters to generate 139 candidate
features, including local demand, competition, accessibility, and site-history
signals.

The XGBoost pipeline combines numeric imputation with target encoding and was
evaluated on **61,823 food-service business records**. Chronological validation trained on
the 2019–2021 cohorts and tested on the 2022 cohort, producing an AUROC of
**0.71**. Future events were excluded from occupancy and business-history
features, and offline feature definitions were aligned with the serving pipeline
to reduce train–serve skew. Rank-preserving calibration converted model outputs
into service probabilities, while TreeSHAP identified the five most influential
factors for each listing.

The live workflow links these components into a single report: users select a
site and business category, review its survival score, and inspect the local
conditions that raised or lowered the estimate. The output is presented as a
comparative planning signal rather than a guarantee of business success.
